Kitchen water damage is a cabinet and subfloor problem

Kitchen water damage restoration in Arroyo Grande, CA is the work of drying and cleaning a kitchen after a leak, then removing the pieces that cannot be saved. A kitchen packs a sink, a dishwasher, a refrigerator line and a stack of swelling materials into one small room.

Most kitchen losses start small. A slow drip at a supply line soaks the particleboard floor of a base cabinet for weeks before anyone sees a stain. The line is answered 24/7 on any day of the week, and the crew visits when it can, so a planned visit can follow a calm phone call.

Where kitchen water hides

What you see at the surface is usually a small part of what is wet underneath.

Under the sink

Supply lines, the trap and the disposal drip onto a cabinet floor that is often particleboard. It swells long before the door looks different.

Behind the dishwasher

A failed hose or door gasket sends water along the subfloor and into the neighboring cabinet. A warped toe-kick is the first clue, and our appliance leak water damage page covers the machine side.

Under the refrigerator

An ice-maker line can leak for a long time behind a heavy appliance. Cupped flooring near the fridge is the tell.

Inside the cabinet toe-kick

Water pools in the narrow gap under the base cabinets, where insulation or drywall behind them stays wet. A moisture meter finds it, a glance does not.

Kitchen safety before anything else

Water, outlets and appliances share a few feet of counter space. Slow down and check these first.

Close the supply valve

Shut the small valve under the sink or behind the appliance. If the leak keeps going, close the main water shutoff.

Respect the outlets

If water reached outlets, the range or the dishwasher wiring, do not touch them. Cut power at the panel only if you can reach it standing on dry floor.

Gas range

If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility before calling us.

Pull the plug on appliances

Unplug the refrigerator and dishwasher only if the cord and the floor in front of them are dry.

Our process in a wet kitchen

  1. 1

    Find the source and stop it

    The crew confirms the valve is closed and the line is no longer feeding the damage. Pipe or appliance repair stays with a plumber.

  2. 2

    Map the moisture

    A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera trace the water through the cabinet bases, wall bottoms and subfloor.

  3. 3

    Extract and remove

    An extraction unit pulls standing water. Swollen toe-kicks, particleboard and wet insulation come out.

  4. 4

    Dry the cavities

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run on the open cabinet bases and wall bottoms. Drying mats can draw moisture from the floor.

  5. 5

    Check and record

    Daily readings show whether materials are trending dry. The equipment stays until the readings say the room is done.

Insurance and kitchen leaks

A sudden failure, such as a burst supply line or a dishwasher hose that lets go, is often covered by a homeowners policy. A leak that dripped for months may be treated as gradual damage and often is not. Check your policy for the exact wording, and ask your insurer what applies.

The California Department of Insurance claims guide says to photograph the damage and keep receipts for materials you buy to protect the home. Take pictures of the cabinet interiors and the floor before anything is pulled out. Hold off on permanent repairs until an adjuster has looked, if you are filing a claim.

Renters should tell the landlord the same day and keep their own photos. About one in three households in Arroyo Grande rents, so the question of who owns the cabinets and the flooring comes up often. Your lease and your renter policy answer it.

Rebuild questions for cabinets and flooring

Drying and rebuild are separate jobs. Once readings are dry, new cabinet bases, toe-kicks, subfloor patches and flooring can go in. Our water damage repair page covers that stage in more detail.

Before a cabinet, plumbing or electrical rebuild, ask the city Community Development Department at City Hall, 300 E Branch Street, whether a permit applies. It handles building and permit questions, and a quick call before demolition saves a stop-work surprise later.

Mistakes that make a kitchen leak worse

  • Wiping the surface and calling it done while the cabinet base stays wet
  • Running a household fan at the cabinet doors, which pushes moisture into the room and not out of the cavity
  • Caulking or re-sealing a countertop before the wall behind it has been checked
  • Pushing the refrigerator back into place before the floor under it has been measured
  • Replacing the dishwasher before a plumber confirms why the old one leaked

Common Questions

Can wet kitchen cabinets be saved?

Often yes if they are solid wood or plywood and the water was clean. Particleboard bases that swelled usually need replacement.

A moisture meter reading on the cabinet box and the wall behind it decides.

How do I know water is trapped under my kitchen floor?

Cupped edges on flooring, a musty smell near the dishwasher, or a soft toe-kick are common signs. A thermal imaging camera and moisture meter confirm it.

Do I have to remove the countertop?

Not always. Laminate and stone often stay if the base cabinets can be dried and the countertop is not loose. Removal happens when water is trapped beneath it.

Is an after-hours kitchen leak something you can help with?

The line is answered 24/7, including nights and weekends. Shut off the supply valve first, then call (805) 291-8940.

Who fixes the leaking dishwasher itself?

A plumber or appliance technician repairs the unit or the supply line. Restoration dries and cleans the damage the leak caused.

Should the plumber come before or after the drying?

Before. The source must be fixed first, or the drying crew is chasing a leak that keeps feeding the damage.
