Does your home even sit on a slab?

Slab leak water damage in Arroyo Grande, CA means harm from a pressurized supply line that fails in or beneath a concrete foundation slab. The water has nowhere to go but up through flooring, along baseboards and into the bottom of the walls. We are not sure whether this fits your house, so check before you assume.

We do not have a local count of homes built on slabs, and we will not guess one. You can tell by looking at the foundation edge outside for a solid concrete curb with no vents or access hatch. You can also ask the city's Community Development Department for the home's permit records, or ask a plumber who has worked on the house.

Signs that water is escaping under the floor

No single sign proves a slab leak. Two or three together are a good reason to call a plumber.

A warm spot on the floor

A patch of tile or wood that feels warm underfoot can point to a hot water line leaking below.

The sound of running water

Hissing or trickling when every faucet and appliance is off suggests water is moving somewhere it should not.

Damp or lifting flooring

Carpet that stays wet near a wall, or boards that cup with no visible source, often trace back to the floor below.

A musty smell

A stale odor in one room, especially near the baseboards, means moisture has been sitting for a while.

Water is surfacing through the floor right now

If water is coming up through the floor as you read this, treat it as urgent. The line is answered 24/7, including nights and weekends, and Same-day response when available. Pressurized supply water keeps flowing until someone closes a valve.

Go to the main shutoff and close it. The supply line is fed by the public system, so the pressure behind the leak does not stop on its own. Once the main is closed, call a plumber or leak-detection specialist, then call us for the wet flooring and walls.

First-hour safety steps

If you smell gas or see water around an outlet, panel or appliance cord, call the utility or 911 before you call us.

Close the main valve

Turn the main supply clockwise until it stops. Open one faucet to relieve line pressure.

Mind the electricity

Cut power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground. Otherwise leave it and call the utility.

Check the water heater

With the main closed, a gas or electric heater can overheat if its tank drains. Turn it to pilot or off if you can do so safely.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, boxes and furniture legs off wet flooring. Photograph everything before you move it.

Call the right people

A plumber finds and fixes the pipe. We handle the water damage. You will usually need both.

Equipment for drying a slab and the floor above it

Concrete holds moisture far longer than drywall, so a slab floor is slow to dry. We use pin and pinless moisture meters for readings, a thermal imaging camera to spot cool wet areas, and LGR dehumidifiers to pull water out of the air. Air movers push dry air across surfaces.

Where flooring sits over trapped water, drying mats and injection systems can pull moisture from under tile, wood or laminate without tearing everything out. Whether those fit your floor depends on what it is made of and how long it stayed wet. We explain the plan before work starts.

Slab leaks and your insurance policy

Coverage depends on the wording of your policy and on how the water got there. The California Department of Insurance guide says homeowners policies do not cover flood but do cover other kinds of water damage. A burst line under a slab is a plumbing event, not a flood.

Sudden and accidental leaks are often covered, while slow leaks from neglect often are not, and the pipe repair may be treated differently from the damage it caused. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep photos, plumber paperwork and receipts together.

Mistakes to avoid with a suspected slab leak

  • Waiting to see if the wet spot dries on its own. A pressurized leak does not stop by itself.
  • Covering the area with a rug or new flooring before the source is found.
  • Running fans alone and calling the floor dry because the surface feels dry.
  • Ignoring a rising water bill or a meter that moves with everything off.
  • Skipping the plumber and hoping restoration crews can fix the pipe. They cannot.

How do I know if my house is on a slab?

Look at the outside edge of the foundation. A solid concrete edge with no vents or crawl hatch suggests a slab. The city's Community Development Department can tell you what permit records exist, and a plumber can confirm it quickly.

Who finds the leak under the floor?

A plumber or leak-detection specialist does. We dry and restore what the water reached, but we do not repair supply pipes.

Can a wet concrete slab be dried?

Yes, but it takes patience. Dehumidifiers and air movers run while we take readings, and the equipment stays until the numbers settle. We do not promise a fixed number of days because it depends on the slab and the conditions.

Will mold grow under my flooring?

It can when moisture sits for a long time. The EPA advises drying water-damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. If you suspect mold already, we cover that on our mold remediation page.

Is a slab leak covered as flood damage?

Usually not. A burst supply line is a plumbing event, and flood coverage is a separate policy. Read your policy or ask your insurer how it treats pipe leaks.
