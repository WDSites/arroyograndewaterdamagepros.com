You cannot judge a mold company by its truck or its website. You can judge it by what it will put in writing.

Color is not the test

Black mold is a phrase people search when they see dark growth on a wall or ceiling. The color alone does not tell anyone what the growth is or how serious it is, and no honest company will diagnose it from a text message. Health questions belong to a physician.

What you can control is who you hire. This guide does not rank or name any firm. It gives you seven checks to run on any company that offers black mold removal in Arroyo Grande, CA, and a list of behaviors that should end the conversation.

Seven checks before you sign

  1. 1

    Ask for a written scope

    It should name the rooms, the materials to be removed or cleaned, the containment and the drying. Verbal promises do not count.

  2. 2

    Ask for the containment plan

    Look for plastic barriers, negative air with HEPA filtration and a described path for bagged waste.

  3. 3

    Ask about the standard

    Ask whether the crew works to ANSI/IICRC S520, the published standard for mold remediation. A confident company answers plainly.

  4. 4

    Separate testing from removal

    Whoever profits from the removal should not be the only one saying the job is clean.

  5. 5

    Ask who actually does the work

    Is it the company's own crew or a subcontractor? Who is on site each day?

  6. 6

    Request references

    Ask for two recent customers with similar jobs, and call them.

  7. 7

    Confirm the source plan

    The proposal should say who stops the moisture and how it will be verified dry.

Red flags worth walking away from

Any one of these is reason to get another proposal.

  • Fear selling: warnings about your family's safety with no moisture readings behind them.
  • No written scope, or a scope with a single line like mold removal.
  • Cash only, or a request for most of the payment before any work starts.
  • Pressure to sign today, with a discount that disappears tomorrow.
  • A promise that mold will never return, which no one can honestly make.
  • A lab report offered from the company that also does the cleanup, with no independent option.
  • Refusal to give a license number or name the person responsible for the job.
  • Spraying or fogging as the whole treatment, without removal of wet porous material.

Arroyo Grande note: check the license, then check it again

California requires a contractor license for work of $1,000 or more in combined labor and materials, effective January 1, 2025. The exemption does not apply if the work needs a permit or the person hires workers.

So ask for the license number in writing, then look it up yourself on the CSLB website. Match the name on the license to the name on the contract. For mold-specific licensing, ask whether your state licenses mold work, and do not assume it does.

Source type: California Contractors State License Board

Questions to put to a reference

When you call a reference, skip the question of whether they were happy. Ask what the scope said, whether the crew followed it, how the site was left each night and whether the final invoice matched the proposal.

Ask if the company returned phone calls during the job and what happened when something unexpected turned up behind a wall. How a company handles a surprise tells you more than how it handles a plan.

Paperwork to keep

Keep the signed scope, every change order, moisture logs, photos from before and after, and any lab report. If a dispute comes up later, those pages carry more weight than memory.

If you plan to file with your insurer, read the policy first. Sudden and accidental water events are often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and some policies carry a separate limit for mold. Check your policy and ask your insurer before you sign, not after.

Ask for the contract to state the start date, the payment schedule, who pulls any permit and what happens if the crew finds more growth than expected. A clause that says additional work needs your written approval keeps the final bill honest.

What a good first visit looks like

A good visit involves instruments. Someone should arrive with a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera, ask about the history of the leak and look at the roof, plumbing or windows that could be the source.

You should leave with a written proposal, a plain explanation of containment and an honest answer if the job is smaller than you feared. A company that discovers a small job and says so has earned some trust.

Questions Homeowners Ask

Is black mold more dangerous than other mold?

That is a medical question, so it belongs with a physician. For the cleanup, the process is the same: find the moisture, contain, remove and verify under ANSI/IICRC S520.

Should the company that tests also do the removal?

It is safer to separate them. An independent tester has no stake in selling you a larger job, and a second pair of eyes confirms the work.

How do I check a contractor's license in California?

Ask for the license number and look it up on the CSLB website. Match the name on the license to the contract. The $1,000 threshold took effect January 1, 2025.

What if the company says it needs cash up front?

Treat that as a red flag. Get a written scope and ask for a payment schedule tied to progress, not a large cash deposit.

Do I need a permit for mold removal in Arroyo Grande?

Rules vary, so ask the City of Arroyo Grande Community Development Department. Note that the CSLB license exemption does not apply when the work needs a permit.

Can I use the same company to dry the house and remove mold?

Often yes, and it can be convenient. Just confirm the scope shows drying, removal and verification as separate line items.
