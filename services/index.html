Which service fits your situation

Water damage work happens in stages, and each service below covers one stage. Water on the floor needs removal. Wet walls need drying. Ruined materials need cleanup, and what remains needs a rebuild. If you are not sure which one you need, call (805) 291-8940 and describe what you see. The dispatcher will place you on the right track.

Most homes need more than one stage. A burst pipe typically moves from extraction to drying to rebuild, and each step is a separate scope with its own price explained before work starts.

Emergency Water Removal

When water is standing in a room, emergency water removal is the first call. Pumps and extraction units pull water out of carpet, pad and flooring before it wicks up the walls. It is the right page for a burst supply line, an overflowing washer or a failed water heater.

If the water came from a toilet, drain or outside, keep people and pets out of it. Shut off the main water valve for a supply break, and turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel dry.

Water Mitigation

Water mitigation is the work of the first 24 to 72 hours: shutting off the source, mapping moisture with meters and thermal imaging cameras, and setting air movers and LGR dehumidifiers. Its goal is to stop damage from spreading into drywall, insulation and subfloor.

Think of it as the stage that decides how much of your house you keep. A crew that dries in place often saves drywall and baseboards that would otherwise be cut out.

Water Damage Cleanup

Once water is out, the job of removing ruined materials begins. Our water damage cleanup service covers debris removal, discarding contaminated carpet pad and drywall, sanitizing and deodorizing, especially after gray or black water.

This is also where water categories matter. Clean supply water, gray water and sewage are handled differently, and the category decides what is cleaned and what is thrown away.

Flood Damage Restoration

Storm, creek and groundwater flooding are different from a plumbing leak because the water carries soil and contamination. Flood damage restoration addresses basements, crawl spaces and sewage backups. Arroyo Grande Creek runs through town, so check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Flood is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies, so ask your insurer whether you carry separate flood coverage before a storm season arrives.

Water Damage Repair

After the structure is dry, water damage repair rebuilds what had to be removed. That means drywall, insulation, flooring, paint, baseboards and cabinets. Repair is its own scope, separate from drying, and it is the stage where finish choices come in.

Hardwood cupping, ceiling stains and wall damage from a roof leak all sit here, once moisture readings confirm the area is dry.

Mold Remediation

Mold sits at the end of the chain, not the start. It needs moisture, and it shows up when a wet area was not dried in time. Mold remediation covers inspection, containment, removal and drying following ANSI/IICRC S520. It makes no health claims, and questions about health belong to a physician.

What to do before you call

These steps apply to every service on this page.

  • Shut off the water at the source or the main valve if you can reach it safely.
  • Do not use outlets or appliances near standing water, and call 911 or the utility for any electrical or gas risk.
  • Move valuables and keep people and pets out of contaminated water.
  • Take photos of the damage before cleanup begins.
  • Call (805) 291-8940 and describe the rooms involved.

Common Questions

Which service should I call for first?

If water is standing, start with removal. If it is gone but walls feel damp, call for mitigation. A dispatcher can sort it out when you call.

Do I need every service?

No. Many small losses need only extraction and drying. Cleanup, rebuild and mold work apply only if the damage calls for them.

Do you offer emergency service?

Yes. The line is answered 24/7. Same-day response when available.

Does insurance cover these services?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

Do you handle fire damage?

No. We focus on water damage and mold after moisture.
