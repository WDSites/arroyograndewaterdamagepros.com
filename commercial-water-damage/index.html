What commercial water damage restoration covers

Commercial water damage restoration in Arroyo Grande, CA is the removal of water from a business or multi-unit building, the drying of walls, ceilings and floors, and the cleaning of every area the water touched. The steps match a house job. The scale does not, because the floor plans are bigger and the walls are shared.

Water usually arrives from a split supply line, a failed roof drain, an overflowing toilet or floor drain, or a rooftop unit that drips into a ceiling grid. The city covers about 5.9 square miles with US 101 and State Route 227 running through it, so a loss can hit a small storefront or a larger building. Find your main water shutoff now and write its location on the wall beside it.

How a business loss differs from a house loss

A commercial job carries more moving parts than one soaked family room. Four of them shape almost every plan.

A bigger footprint

Water follows hallways, drop ceilings and shared walls. A leak in one suite can reach three others before anyone notices the stain.

More than one party

An owner, a tenant and a property manager may each hold a piece of the problem. Who pays for what is a lease question, not a drying question.

Operating hours

Open doors, customers and staff change how extraction and drying equipment can be placed. Tell us what must stay usable.

Records

Insurers and owners want dated photos, moisture readings and a written scope. Good records start on day one.

Building safety in the first hour

Work through these points while you wait. The line takes the call any day of the week and the crew visits when it can, so do only what is safe.

  • If water is near outlets, panels, light fixtures or a ceiling that sags, call the utility or 911 before you call us. Electrical and gas risks come first.
  • Close the building's main water shutoff. For a sprinkler or fire line, call the owner's fire protection contractor, because that line is not ours to touch.
  • Cut power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry floor. If the panel is wet, leave it alone.
  • Clear people out of wet corridors and away from sagging ceiling tiles. Wet floors are slippery.
  • Lift inventory, files, computers and anything valuable off the floor. Photograph the water and the damage before you move things.

Our process, from first walk-through to final readings

The crew follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published industry standard for water damage restoration. Ask any company whether its people work to it.

  1. 1

    Walk the building

    We trace the water from its source and record wet areas with moisture meters. A thermal imaging camera shows wetness behind finishes.

  2. 2

    Remove the water

    Extraction units pull standing water from carpet and hard floors. Larger areas take more units and more passes.

  3. 3

    Take out what cannot dry

    Saturated ceiling tile, insulation, carpet pad and swollen baseboards come out so air can reach the structure.

  4. 4

    Dry the structure

    Air movers push air across surfaces while LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the room. Large spaces may need more equipment. See water mitigation for the early-hours work.

  5. 5

    Clean and check

    Affected areas get cleaned and deodorized. Daily moisture readings show when each material is dry and the equipment can leave.

Business insurance and who covers what

Commercial policies differ a great deal from home policies. Whether the building, the contents or lost income is covered depends on the wording, so check your policy or ask your insurer or broker. Business interruption is a question only your insurer can answer.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while a slow leak that went unfixed often is not. Flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. If you rent, your lease sets who insures the walls and who insures what is inside them, so read it with your landlord or property manager.

Mistakes to avoid after a commercial water loss

  • Waiting to see whether the stain spreads. Wet ceiling tile and insulation hold water long after the visible leak stops.
  • Running household fans only. They move air, but they do not pull moisture out of a building the way dehumidifiers do.
  • Throwing out damaged stock, finishes or fixtures before photographing them. Your insurer may ask to see them.
  • Reopening a wet area too soon. Check readings first, and keep customers out of rooms with equipment running.

What we do and what we do not do

We dispatch licensed and insured restoration professionals to commercial buildings for water removal, structural drying, cleanup of affected areas and coordination with the building owner. We also arrange the rebuild of what was removed. For smaller nearby jobs, see our Pismo Beach and San Luis Obispo pages.

Common Questions

Who pays for commercial water damage, the landlord or the tenant?

That depends on your lease and on each party's insurance policy. Read the lease and call your insurer or broker before you decide who pays.

Can you work around our business hours?

Tell us when the space needs to stay open and which areas matter most. We scope and price the work before it starts, and we plan equipment placement around what you share.

How long does drying take in a commercial space?

It depends on the materials, the amount of water and the equipment on site. Readings decide when the job is done, not a calendar.

Will I need a permit for the rebuild?

Drying and cleanup are separate from rebuild work. For rebuilding, ask the Community Development Department at Arroyo Grande City Hall whether your repairs need a permit.
