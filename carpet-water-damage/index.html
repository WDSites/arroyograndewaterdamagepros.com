Carpet water damage is a three-layer problem

Carpet water damage restoration in Arroyo Grande, CA means pulling water out of the carpet, the cushion pad and the subfloor below them, then drying all three until they read dry on a moisture meter. The carpet face is only the visible layer. The pad acts like a sponge underneath, and the tack strip along the walls holds moisture against the baseboard.

Most calls start with a washing machine hose, a water heater drip, a toilet supply line or a rain leak through a wall. This page is for planned decisions about the carpet itself. The 24/7 line takes the call any day of the week, and the crew visits when it can.

Carpet water safety before anyone steps in

If water reached an outlet, a baseboard heater or a ceiling fixture, call the electric utility or 911 before you call us. Do not stand on wet carpet near a panel or power strip.

Stop the source

Close the fixture shutoff or the main water valve. A carpet that keeps getting wet will never dry.

Cut power to wet rooms only if safe

Use the breaker only if you can reach the panel on dry floor.

Lift what you can

Raise furniture legs onto foil or blocks, and move books, shoes and electronics to dry ground.

Treat sewage as off limits

If the water came from a drain or toilet backup, keep children and pets out and wear boots, gloves and goggles if you must enter.

Carpet drying steps from extraction to reinstall

Crews follow the drying approach published as ANSI/IICRC S500, and you can ask any company whether its techs work to that standard.

  1. 1

    Extract

    A truck-mounted or portable extraction unit pulls water up through the face. Slow passes remove far more than a wet vacuum.

  2. 2

    Map the damage

    A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show how far the water wicked under walls and into the tack strip.

  3. 3

    Lift and float

    The carpet is peeled back from a corner, the pad is cut out or dried, and the carpet is supported on air so it dries from both sides.

  4. 4

    Dry with air and dehumidifiers

    Air movers push air under the carpet while an LGR dehumidifier pulls the moisture out of the room.

  5. 5

    Clean and treat

    Antimicrobial treatment and deodorizing go on when the water was not clean. Readings are checked daily.

  6. 6

    Stretch and reinstall

    Once the subfloor reads dry, the carpet is re-stretched on new or cleaned tack strip and set back on fresh pad.

Pad and subfloor decide how the job ends

Rebond foam pad compresses and soaks up water, and it dries slowly. Many crews replace it even when the carpet stays, because new pad costs little next to a second visit. Plywood and particleboard subfloors can swell at the seams, and a cupped panel under a dry carpet will show as a ripple later.

Check for a musty smell once the room is back together. The EPA says water-damaged areas should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to head off mold growth, so a damp carpet that has sat longer deserves a closer look at the pad underneath. Ask for the final readings of the carpet edge, tack strip and subfloor.

Insurance and carpet claims

Sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst supply line or a failed hose, is often covered by a homeowners policy. Slow seepage and long-neglected leaks often are not. Flood damage is usually excluded and needs a separate flood policy, so check your policy before assuming anything either way.

Photograph the wet carpet before it is moved, keep the pad scraps if your insurer asks for them, and ask your insurer what it needs before permanent repairs begin. Mistakes to avoid with wet carpet

  • Rolling the carpet up hot and wet and leaving it in the garage, where it stays soaked and starts to smell.
  • Walking across wet carpet in street shoes, which grinds soil into the backing and spreads contamination.
  • Steam cleaning alone, which adds hot water to a pad that is still holding water.
  • Running a household fan and calling it dry, when only meter readings can show the pad and subfloor are done.
  • Pulling up carpet touched by sewage without gloves, boots and goggles.

Common Questions

How long does wet carpet take to dry?

It depends on the water source, the pad and how many air movers and dehumidifiers are running. Daily moisture readings, not a fixed number of days, tell the crew when to stop.

Is it okay to keep the carpet but replace only the pad?

Often, yes, when the water was clean and the carpet backing is intact. The pad holds water longest, so replacing it is common even when the carpet is saved.

Can I steam clean flooded carpet myself?

Steam cleaning does not extract the water trapped in the pad and can add moisture. Extraction first, then drying, then cleaning is the safer order.

Does carpet touched by sewage get cleaned?

No. The CDC says rugs and cloth items that cannot be disinfected should be thrown away, and wall-to-wall carpet falls in that group when sewage soaked it.

Do you come out at night for a soaked carpet?

The 24/7 line takes the call any hour. Same-day response when available, including nights and weekends, and the crew visits when it can.

What should I do if my rented home has wet carpet?

Photograph it, stop the source if you safely can, and tell your landlord in writing. Ask whose insurance applies before anything is thrown out.
