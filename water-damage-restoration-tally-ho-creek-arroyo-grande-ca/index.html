What happened here in 2023

This page covers the area along Tally Ho Creek, which local news used when reporting creek flooding in Arroyo Grande in early 2023. Television station KSBY reported that a January 9, 2023 storm flooded homes along Tally Ho Road, and that the City of Arroyo Grande declared a local emergency afterward. Several homes were reported uninhabitable.

On March 10, 2023, the city announced an evacuation warning for both sides of Tally Ho Creek from Corbett Canyon Road to James Way. CalCoastNews reported it ran from 11 a.m. Friday to 9 a.m. Saturday. KSBY later quoted residents who said the road flooded again that week.

These reports describe particular storms. They do not show that any given home will flood, and they do not tell us how a particular street is mapped. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified.

When creek or storm water is spreading in your home

Treat creek water as contaminated. It can carry sediment, runoff and sewage. Keep people and pets out of it, do not wade through it to reach a panel, and call 911 or the utility for any electrical or gas risk.

When it is safe to do so, call (805) 291-8940. The line is open 24/7. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for the Tally Ho Creek area, including during storm weeks, though no arrival time is promised. Same-day response when available.

During active flooding, follow instructions from local officials. The county asks residents to sign up for AlertSLO.org notices and to use ReadySLO.org for storm preparedness.

Before We Arrive

Leave if officials tell you to

An evacuation warning or order comes before any cleanup. Restoration waits until the area is safe.

Stay out of standing creek water

Do not walk through it, and do not run fans or vacuums in it.

Do not use breakers while wet

If the panel is in a wet area, leave it and call the utility.

Photograph the waterline

Take pictures of high-water marks on walls, fences and contents when it is safe.

Call the 24/7 line

How a creek flood job runs

  1. 1

    Assess the water category

    Creek water is treated as contaminated, so the crew sets containment and personal protection first.

  2. 2

    Pump and extract

    Pumps and extraction units remove water, then mud and sediment are shoveled or vacuumed from floors.

  3. 3

    Remove what cannot be saved

    Wet carpet, pad and porous materials that held contaminated water usually come out. Drywall is cut to a measured line.

  4. 4

    Clean and dry

    Surfaces are cleaned and sanitized, then air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings return to dry.

  5. 5

    Keep the records

    Photos, readings and a written scope support your insurance conversation. See flood damage restoration for the full sequence.

Safety guidance for contaminated water

The CDC advises against entering floodwater and warns that it may contain sewage and chemicals. Ordinary household fans and vacuums are not enough for contaminated water. Do not eat food that touched it, and wear protective gloves and boots if you must handle wet items.

Mold can follow if wet materials stay damp. The EPA says drying within 24 to 48 hours helps limit mold growth. We make no health claims, so ask a physician about any symptoms. If growth appears, see mold remediation.

Cost, insurance and the paperwork

Cost depends on the depth and category of the water, the area affected, how much sediment came in, which materials must be removed and how long drying takes. We explain the scope before work starts and quote no price by phone.

Flood damage is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies and needs separate flood coverage, while a sudden plumbing failure is treated differently. The California Department of Insurance guide says to document the loss and keep receipts. Check your policy and ask your insurer. We cannot determine coverage.

Common Questions

Is emergency water removal available near Tally Ho Creek?

Yes, the line is answered 24/7. When officials have closed an area during active flooding, crews cannot enter it, and arrival times are never guaranteed.

Which part of Arroyo Grande does this page cover?

It covers the creek corridor residents and local news call the Tally Ho Creek area, in ZIP 93420. The reports named Tally Ho Road and the stretch from Corbett Canyon Road to James Way. Call with your address to confirm.

Is my home in a FEMA flood zone?

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here. Check your exact address in FEMA's Flood Map Service Center. A mapped zone does not predict flooding, and an unmapped one does not rule it out.

Does homeowners insurance cover creek flooding?

Flood is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

Can you stop the creek from flooding my home again?

No. Restoration repairs damage and is not drainage or creek work. Ask the city or county about creek maintenance.
